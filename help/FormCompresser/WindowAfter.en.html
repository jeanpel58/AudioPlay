⏭️Window After (seconds)

This setting controls how many seconds of audio are analyzed AFTER the TOC (the point recorded in the disc table, usually the start of the next track) when searching for transitions between tracks.

What is the TOC?

The TOC (Table of Contents) on an audio CD is a small table written to the disc that lists the start positions of each track (in minutes:seconds:frames) and the lead-out position. It is a logical index used by CD players. The TOC does not describe the actual waveform around those positions: it does not tell you if tracks overlap, contain crossfades, or have silence. That is why analyzing the audio around the TOC is necessary to find the safe silence where cutting is appropriate.

Why it exists

Sometimes the true end of a track is after the TOC due to hidden lead-out audio or if the next track starts later. Analyzing after the TOC helps detect silences or fades that occur just after the boundary.

Effect of changing it

  • Smaller value: faster, may miss short post-TOC silences.
  • Larger value: examines more audio and may find a better cut, at the cost of time.
Recommendation: default conservative value: 20 seconds. Use 10–30s for albums with long lead-outs.
